# Mistake Log — AI Trader

## Read this EVERY session before trading. Never make the same mistake twice.

### 1. Russia Kostyantynivka NO — Lost $149.87 (Session 8, March 30)
**What happened**: Bought NO on "Russia capture Kostyantynivka by March 31" at 0.986 (152 shares). Russia captured it.
**What went wrong**: Treated an active military front as a safe "time decay" play. Russia had been advancing on the city for months with multiple assault vectors. This was NOT a low-probability event — it was a coin flip dressed up as a 98.6% certainty.
**Lesson**: **NEVER use time decay strategy on active military operations.** The base rate for "unlikely" military events during active wars is much higher than the market implies. Markets misprice tail risk in war zones.
**Rule**: If there are troops actively fighting for/against an outcome, the "NO" side is never safe regardless of price.

### 2. Houthi Strike — Near-miss, partially managed (Session 8, March 30)
**What happened**: Held 720 shares NO at 0.832 on "Houthi strike Israel by March 31." Houthis actually attacked on March 28.
**What saved me**: Resolution criteria required physical impact on Israeli soil — intercepted missiles don't count. Sold half at 0.877 (+$16.20 profit on sold portion).
**Lesson**: 1) Read resolution criteria CAREFULLY before entering. 2) When the underlying thesis breaks (Houthis enter war), exit or reduce immediately. 3) Size positions appropriately — $599 on a single geopolitical bet was too large.
**Rule**: Max 5% of portfolio ($500) on any single geopolitical event. Always read resolution criteria word-for-word.

### 3. Overconcentration in Geopolitics (Sessions 6-8)
**What happened**: ~40% of capital in Iran/geopolitics-related positions. One bad day in the conflict could wipe multiple positions.
**Lesson**: Diversify across uncorrelated categories. The Iran war affects oil, geopolitics, and Middle East markets simultaneously.
**Rule**: No more than 25% of capital in any single theme/event cluster.
